# Elevator pitch

Every sentence maps to something visible in the demo; nothing is claimed that
the screen does not show.

## One-liner (~10 s)

When reality changes, the right agents wake up. GlassWake detects enterprise
memory rot, repairs it with bounded authority, and refuses to close until an
independent verifier proves the fix.

## Elevator pitch (~30 s — video opener)

Companies don't fail because nobody wrote the truth down. They fail because the
truth changed in one place and stayed stale everywhere else. Northstar changed
its returns policy from 30 to 14 days; the storefront never got the memo.
GlassWake wakes only the agents responsible for what might now be wrong: five
surfaces revalidated, seven safely skipped. A deterministic authority gate
grants one bounded repair. GlassWake executes it as a real HTTPS action against
a separate service, and an independent verifier re-reads that service before
the receipt can seal. A model can propose. It cannot grant itself authority,
and it cannot certify its own work.

## Closer (~10 s — over the corrected storefront)

Most agents are designed to remember. GlassWake notices when what they remember
is no longer true.

## Narration anchors for the recorded take

- External action: "Not a state transition — an authenticated mutation against

- Verification: "The action's own success response is never trusted as proof."
- Executive brief: "The spoken text is assembled from receipt fields; Gemma,
